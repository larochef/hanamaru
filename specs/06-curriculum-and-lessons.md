# 06 — Curriculum and lessons

**Status:** built: units (a point, a theme, its words, its symbols), 71 points covering N5, N4 and the start of N3, lessons with vocabulary, reading texts, exercises, placement and sessions

## Intent

A steady progression through grammar, vocabulary and kanji, where each lesson
can be repeated with fresh content until it is acquired.

## Decided

- **A fixed curriculum skeleton**: an ordered list of grammar points, roughly
  100 to 150, from N5 towards N3. Each point has a short French explanation,
  its prerequisites, and the vocabulary and kanji its lesson introduces. It is
  written once, reviewed, and kept as a resource file in the repository. The AI
  fills lessons with content but never decides the order.
- **The curriculum file** is `backend/resources/curriculum/grammar.json`: the
  points in the order they are learned, each with an id, its JLPT level, a
  title and a pattern (in kana only: the learner reads no kanji yet), the
  points it relies on, its explanation (French paragraphs), its examples
  (written as a Japanese would, with their French translation), the words it
  introduces besides those of its examples (はい, いいえ…), whether it has
  reading texts, its two placement questions ([`04`](04-placement.md)), and
  its **signature**: the token sequences that show the point in a sentence, each
  token described by its dictionary form, its part of speech and its
  conjugated form as Sudachi names them (`ます` as `終止形` for the present,
  `ます` then `ぬ` for ません). The signatures are what lets code tell the
  grammar of a sentence ([`07`](07-text-generation.md)).
- **Written, not generated**: the explanations and examples are written in the
  repository, in their own words, not taken from a textbook, and not
  generated by the tutor. The order follows the usual progression of beginner
  courses, with one constraint checked by a test: an example shows its own
  point and no point that comes after it, the examples analysed by Sudachi
  (the test needs its dictionary in the cache, else it says so and skips
  them). A point relies only on points before it.
- **Units**: grammar and vocabulary go together. Each point is a unit with a
  theme (the town, the family, numbers and the hour, colours, the weather…)
  and 10 to 18 words, written in the curriculum file as they are written
  (`上|うえ` when the form alone is ambiguous, `コップ#1050390` when not even
  the reading is: homonyms in kana, the glass and not the cop), found in
  JMdict (a test checks every one, and prints which entry each found, to be
  read for such homonyms). The lesson shows its words; its texts are asked to use
  them; the vocabulary series practise the unit under way
  ([`08`](08-vocabulary-practice.md)). A unit is **acquired** once its point
  is practised (its card past its first steps), 80% of its words are
  stable and its symbols learned (below). **Moving on is the learner's**:
  an acquired unit stays the unit under way, with a word of congratulation
  and the choice to move on to the next or to go on with it (its words then
  come in turn in the series), until the learner moves on; the units right
  after it acquired too (a placement, practice ahead) are left with it. The
  units left are kept (`unit_left`); the unit under way is the first not
  left. The next unit's words open only then. The units' words, as
  written, hold every kanji of the first two school grades (240), so that
  each can be learned through a word already known (a test checks it).
- **A unit's symbols** (built): what it teaches to read and write. The first
  unit's are the hiragana, the second's the katakana: a beginner learns them
  there, a few each session, and the first unit's words wait until its
  hiragana are all met (they are written with them); the second's do not
  wait for its katakana (they are not written with them). A kanji is
  assigned only through a word usually written with it (分かる, usually in
  kana, carries no 分): the same words it is then practised in, so that every
  unit's kanji can be met (a test checks it). From the third unit on,
  its kanji: those of the first two grades, in the order the
  units' words bring them, each in the first unit whose words have it, four
  per unit at most, the rest carried on to the next units: the first units'
  words bring far more (13 in the first) than later ones (none in some). The
  first two units bring none, while the kana are still being settled; theirs
  start with the third. So a kanji comes some units after its words, which
  are first learned in kana; all 240 are placed by the last unit.
  A unit is acquired once its symbols are learned too, each read and written
  past its first steps; they are practised in My lesson
  ([`09`](09-sessions.md)).
- **Passed once, passed**: a word, a point or a symbol counts as past its
  first steps once its card has left them, even if a later lapse sends it
  back to relearning: a unit acquired never closes again, and the lapse is
  only the card's, reviewed again as FSRS says.
- **Granularity**: one point per particle or form a beginner meets as one
  thing (は, の, ます, ません and ました together, ではありません…). The 71
  points: です, は, か, の, も, これ/この/ここ, ます, を, へ/に,
  ません/ました/でした, ではありません, the い and な adjectives, で, ある/いる
  (and が), the hour (に), the adjectives' negative and past, くて/で, たい,
  ましょう/ませんか, the て form and てください, ている, the dictionary form
  and ことができる, the ない form and ないでください, the た form and
  たことがある, the plain style (だ, ね, よ), から/まで, から (because), と/や,
  comparison (より, のほうが, いちばん), とき, まえに/あとで, てもいい/てはいけない,
  あげる/もらう/くれる, つもり, なる; と思う, と言う, でしょう/かもしれない,
  the potential form, the volitional form and ようと思う, たら, なければならない,
  たほうがいい, てしまう, てみる, ておく, そう (looks), ながら, ので; てある,
  the passive, the causative, すぎる, やすい/にくい, ば, と (whenever), のに,
  けど/が, ために, ように, ようになる/ようにする, たり, なくてもいい,
  そう (hearsay), ようだ/みたい/らしい; then N3, a first batch: たばかり,
  ところ (about to, in the middle of, just done), はず, ことにする/ことになる,
  ように言う (said to do).
- **Placement answers in kana** may mislead the analyser (よめ, ねよう): a
  question then gives its answer as written (`written`: 読め), for the test.
- **What a point names is grammar**: once a point is learned, the words its
  signature names by their form (できる, しまう, みる, the potential verbs
  listed) are not unknown words in a text; what it describes by kind (any
  adjective) still is.
  The negation taught is ではありません, as written; じゃありません is shown
  as its spoken contraction, and accepted in exercises with a remark.
  を and へ/に come right after ます: without them, verbs make no text.
- **The lesson page** (built): the explanation, the Japanese in it set apart,
  then the examples laid out in kana by the analyser, words spaced, the
  grammar of the point marked in red, each with its translation; the points it
  relies on, and the previous and next points, as links; a **reading text**
  written on demand by the tutor ([`07`](07-text-generation.md)), each
  sentence's translation folded away. Any word, in the examples or the text,
  is tapped for its dictionary form, reading and meanings (JMdict's French,
  then its first English sense). ます has no text: with only it learned, a
  text of several sentences cannot be made.
- **Exercises** (built), asked on the lesson page, in the sentences of the
  point's examples and of a new reading text that show it, each asked once:
  - **the particles typed** (when the point has a particle): the sentence
    with a blank for every particle learned so far it has, the point's and
    the earlier ones' (in the lesson of の: わたし＿ ちち＿ いしゃです), each
    blank a kana field (romaji turned into hiragana as it is typed), or
    written with the stylus in the blank chosen (the one last gone to,
    marked), its French under it; and, besides
    the lesson's sentences, two from the examples of the particles learned
    before (は, か in the lesson of の). A choice among four would give the
    answer away, always the point's: typed, every particle is thought of;
  - **the sentence translated** from French, typed in kana, the script
    expected said under it ("in hiragana, with one word in katakana"); the
    kana field has a switch, あ or ア, for the script it types in; or written
    with the stylus, kana after kana, each recognised and put in the field
    ([`05`](05-writing.md)), and the kanji the learner writes, the field
    switched to them (あ, ア, 漢). Right in
    the other script, the answer is right, and the words in katakana named.
    A word written with kanji, all or some (会しゃ), is read back as its kana
    before the answer is compared; right all in kana, the words the learner
    writes with kanji are recalled, as they write them.

  - **the form chosen** (built): for the points of a verb's polite
    endings (ます, ません, ました, ませんでした, ましょう, たい), the ending
    blanked as one, chosen among four learned by then (none while ます is
    the only one): the French says which (tense, negation, wish, invitation);
  - **the meaning given** (built): a sentence shown in Japanese, its meaning
    typed in French, in sentences whose French the series has not shown yet
    (chosen or translated: that would give it away); right by the code when it is the French expected (case,
    accents and punctuation aside), else judged by the tutor; the tutor away,
    the learner is shown the meaning and says whether theirs is the same;
  - **a kanji written** (built): in a sentence, a word whose kanji's writing
    is being learned, shown in kana, its kanji written from memory: a review
    of the kanji's writing ([`09`](09-sessions.md)), only when it is due,
    not counted in the point's.

  The page sends how each exercise went; the server counts them (a kanji
  written left out) and records the point's review.

  Four sentences of particles or forms chosen at most (particles first),
  three translations, two meanings, two kanji. The code judges what it can:
  the particles expected, each in its blank (は, へ, を typed as they sound
  are wrong, the spelling explained); the form expected; the sentence expected, whatever its spaces, punctuation
  and script, and は, へ, を typed as they sound (right, the spelling
  explained); a sentence right but for at most two slips within its nouns,
  as the analyser reads them (にほん for にほんご, たなかさ for たなかさん),
  right, the slip said: the exercises review the point, not the words around
  it. Its point, particles, endings and conjugated words take no slip: one
  there is a mistake of grammar (が for は, ません for ます). Anything else goes to the tutor (`ExerciseJudge`), who says
  whether it is right all the same (に for へ, another wording, a subject left
  out), corrects the learner's sentence as little as possible, and explains in
  French; its verdicts are shown as the tutor's. Its correction is laid out in
  kana by the analyser. The exercises done are recorded as one review of the
  point ([`03`](03-learner-model.md)). Judged in 1.5 to 2 s; the exercises
  take as long as a text to prepare, about 6 s.
- **Kanji follow the school grades** (80 in grade 1, then the next grades),
  spread across the units by their words (above). The grades after the
  second come with the units still to write, towards N3.
- **A lesson** is:
  - **Grammar**: the point explained in French, with examples.
  - **Vocabulary**: 5 to 15 new words, added to the learner model.
  - **Kanji** (once that phase starts): 2 to 5 new ones, with writing practice.
  - **Text**: a reading text built on the point and the vocabulary
    ([`07`](07-text-generation.md)). Each word can be tapped for its reading and
    meaning.
  - **Exercises**: multiple choice, fill in the blank, translation both ways,
    free answers corrected by the tutor, kanji dictation with the stylus,
    listening and reading aloud when the context allows.
- **Repeating a lesson** brings a new text and new exercises on the same point
  and vocabulary. A lesson is *acquired* when its items are stable in the
  learner model, not after a set number of repetitions.
- **Explanations are in French.** The skeleton's explanations are reviewed text;
  the tutor adds explanations on demand ("why は and not が here?").

## Open

- The rest of the curriculum, towards N3: written in batches, the learner
  reading each batch for clarity (the order is not theirs to judge). Points
  beyond the learner's level, the first N3 batch (たばかり to ように言う),
  are proofread apart, not by the learner. A new point's
  signature makes its pattern "not learned yet" for the texts of every lesson
  before it: a signature too wide would refuse their sentences, so each is
  kept tight (ばかり after た, ところ after a verb or た and before です or
  だ: not the place of 静かなところです). The た of a past is told by its
  conjugation type, 助動詞-タ: its dictionary form may be だ (読んだ), as is
  the な of 静かな or the なら of 静かなら.
- What the signatures do not tell apart yet, found writing the first points:
  いい is an い adjective the analyser calls dependent (非自立可能), and 元気 or
  便利 are nouns that can be adjectives (形状詞可能), so the adjective
  signatures miss them and the examples avoid them; に is any に, not only
  where one goes. では, which the analyser reads as the particles で and は,
  is told apart by its context: a pattern may say what must not follow it
  (the で of a place is not followed by は then ある).
- Exercises still to come: free answers, listening; forms beyond the polite
  endings (the plain forms, the adjectives'), which need a conjugation of
  their own to offer choices.
